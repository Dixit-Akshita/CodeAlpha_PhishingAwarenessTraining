============================================================ PHISHING AWARENESS QUIZ ============================================================ CodeAlpha Cyber Security Internship Task 2 – Phishing Awareness Training Test your ability to identify common phishing and social engineering techniques. ------------------------------------------------------------ QUESTION 1 — The Look-Alike Domain ------------------------------------------------------------ You receive an email that appears to come from your university. The link looks like: https://university-portal.com You normally use: https://university.edu What is the safest conclusion? A. Both links are safe because they contain the university name. B. The first link may be legitimate because it uses HTTPS. C. The domain is different, so the link should be verified through the official university website. D. The first link is safe if the email contains the university logo. Answer: C Explanation: The important part of a URL is the actual registered domain. A familiar name inside a URL does not prove that the website belongs to the organization. ------------------------------------------------------------ QUESTION 2 — The Helpful "IT Support" ------------------------------------------------------------ Someone claiming to be from your company's IT team calls you and says: "We detected suspicious activity on your account. Please tell me your OTP so I can secure it." What should you do? A. Provide the OTP because the caller knows your name. B. Ask the caller to send a verification email and then provide it. C. Refuse to share the OTP and contact IT through an official channel. D. Give only the first few digits of the OTP. Answer: C Explanation: Legitimate support staff should not require you to disclose one-time authentication codes. Verify unexpected requests through a trusted, independent channel. ------------------------------------------------------------ QUESTION 3 — The Unexpected Document ------------------------------------------------------------ You receive an email from an unfamiliar sender with the subject: "Salary_Revision_2026.xlsx" You were not expecting any salary-related document. What is the safest action? A. Open it in Excel and check the contents. B. Download it and scan it later. C. Verify the sender and expected document through another channel. D. Forward it to a colleague to check. Answer: C Explanation: Unexpected attachments can be used to deliver malware or lead to further attacks. Verification should happen before opening the file. ------------------------------------------------------------ QUESTION 4 — The CEO Request ------------------------------------------------------------ You receive a message that appears to be from your CEO: "I'm in a meeting. I need you to purchase gift cards immediately and send me the codes." The email address looks slightly different from the CEO's usual address. What technique is most likely being used? A. Social engineering and impersonation B. Software update C. Password hashing D. Network scanning Answer: A Explanation: Attackers may impersonate executives and create urgency or authority pressure to make employees act without verification. ------------------------------------------------------------ QUESTION 5 — The QR Code Trap ------------------------------------------------------------ You receive an unexpected message containing a QR code with: "Scan now to prevent your account from being suspended." Why should you be cautious? A. QR codes can never be used for phishing. B. The QR code may redirect you to a malicious or fake website. C. QR codes automatically protect your device. D. Scanning a QR code always opens the official website. Answer: B Explanation: Attackers can use QR codes to redirect victims to phishing pages. Always verify the destination before entering credentials or other sensitive information. ============================================================ ANSWER SUMMARY ============================================================ 1. C — Verify the actual domain 2. C — Never share OTPs; verify through official channels 3. C — Verify unexpected attachments 4. A — Social engineering and impersonation 5. B — QR codes can lead to phishing websites ============================================================ KEY TAKEAWAY ============================================================ STOP → CHECK → VERIFY → PROTECT → REPORT When a message creates urgency, fear, curiosity, or pressure, slow down and verify it before taking action. ============================================================
